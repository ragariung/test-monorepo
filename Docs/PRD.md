# Insurance Product Discovery & Application MVP — PRD

**Document type:** Product Requirements Document
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** MVP / Concept-to-build
**Target market:** Indonesia
**Primary channel:** Public web + authenticated internal admin
**Production readiness:** Not production-ready; intended as an MVP/prototype foundation
**Prepared:** 2026-09-04

---

## 1. Product summary

The product is a lightweight digital insurance discovery and lead/application platform for an insurance company.

The MVP has two surfaces:

1. **Public website** where prospective customers can browse insurance products, understand coverage and eligibility, run a premium illustration, and submit an application/registration request.
2. **Admin portal** where internal users can see registrations generated by calls-to-action, inspect submitted information, assign/reassign cases through a manager hierarchy, update application status, and maintain insurance product content.

The product is deliberately **not** a full digital insurance purchasing or underwriting platform. The MVP stops at the point where a prospective customer has submitted an application/registration and the internal team has a traceable workflow for reviewing it.

### Core business proposition

> Turn product discovery into a qualified insurance lead/application through a simple premium illustration and a controlled internal follow-up workflow.

### Primary conversion funnel

`Product discovery → Product detail → Premium illustration → CTA → Applicant information → Submission → Admin review → Approved / Rejected`

## 2. Problem definitions

### 2.1 Customer problems

Prospective insurance customers commonly face information overload. Established insurers structure their public product pages around product categories, customer needs, key benefits, eligibility, premium/payment rules, and clear next actions rather than presenting a raw policy document.

Examples observed in the benchmark:

- Allianz presents a portfolio of individual-life products and uses concise positioning for products such as SmartLink Protection Life, Allianz LegacyPro, Allianz PASTI, and Allianz Future Income, followed by contact/agent CTAs. [1]
- AXA groups personal life products and communicates benefits such as life protection, selectable premium/sum-assured options, and flexible coverage/payment periods, then exposes individual product pages with eligibility and payment details. [2][3]
- Prudential’s PRUMapan page combines product positioning, benefit highlights, detailed eligibility/premium rules, downloadable product documents, and a lead/contact form with personal information and preferred contact time. [4]

This indicates an MVP should answer, in order:

1. What is the product?
2. Who is it for?
3. What does it protect/provide?
4. What affects the premium?
5. What are the eligibility constraints?
6. What should the customer do next?

### 2.2 Business problems

The insurer needs a structured mechanism to capture intent generated by the public website.

Without a central workflow:

- marketing CTAs become disconnected from sales follow-up;
- customer requests may be lost in email/chat/manual spreadsheets;
- there is no consistent application status;
- managers cannot see ownership or workload;
- product content may become stale or inconsistent;
- the business cannot measure the funnel from product view → simulation → registration → review.

### 2.3 MVP problem statement

Build the smallest end-to-end system that proves that a prospective customer can move from insurance-product discovery to a trackable application record, while internal staff can process that record through a controlled approval workflow.

## 3. Solution

### 3.1 Public experience

Create a polished, responsive insurance website with:

- Home / landing page
- Product catalogue
- Product detail page
- Premium simulator / illustration
- Application / registration form
- Submission confirmation

The public site should be educational and conversion-oriented rather than attempting full e-commerce or digital underwriting.

### 3.2 Admin experience

Create an internal admin portal with:

- Login
- Dashboard
- Lead / application inbox
- Application detail view
- Application status workflow
- Assignment / disposition based on manager hierarchy
- Product management
- Product pricing/simulation rule management
- Basic user and role management
- Audit trail

### 3.3 Product-content model

Each insurance product should be represented as structured content rather than a hard-coded page.

Recommended MVP product content:

- Product name
- Short value proposition
- Product type/category
- Hero image
- Key benefits
- Coverage / sum assured description
- Eligibility / age range
- Coverage term options
- Payment term options
- Payment frequency options
- Minimum / maximum values where relevant
- Premium simulation eligibility and rate model
- Important exclusions or limitations summary
- Available brochure / RIPLAY / product document links
- CTA label
- Status: Draft / Published / Archived
- Last updated date

This reflects patterns visible on the benchmark sites, where products expose structured benefit, eligibility, payment, coverage and documentation information. Prudential, for example, exposes both detailed product rules and links to brochures / product summaries, while AXA exposes specific term, payment and entry-age combinations on product pages. [3][4]

### 3.4 Premium simulator

The simulator is an **illustrative calculation**, not a binding quote and not an underwriting decision.

Minimum input requested by this MVP:

- Age
- Sum assured / uang pertanggungan
- Payment term / masa pembayaran premi

Optional configurable dimensions for future expansion:

- Gender
- Smoking status
- Payment frequency
- Product plan
- Coverage term
- Currency
- Rider selection

The simulator should return:

- Estimated premium per selected payment frequency
- Estimated annual premium
- Selected sum assured
- Selected payment term
- Assumption / illustration notice
- CTA to continue to application

The underlying rate must be configurable by administrators rather than encoded in the frontend.

### 3.5 Why the simulator is an illustration

Actual insurance premiums can depend on product-specific eligibility, underwriting, benefit configuration, payment frequency and other rules. The benchmarked products demonstrate materially different rating/eligibility structures: e.g. AXA Term Protector exposes different terms and payment periods, while Prudential PRUMapan has age-based minimum premiums and underwriting thresholds. [3][4]

Therefore the MVP must never imply that a simulated amount is a final policy premium unless an approved production rating engine is later integrated.

### 3.6 Application / registration

The CTA should convert a simulation or product-view intent into a lead/application record.

Minimum fields:

- Full name
- Email
- Phone number
- Age
- Selected product
- Selected sum assured
- Selected payment term
- Simulated premium
- City / domicile
- Preferred contact time
- Optional notes
- Consent to data processing and contact

The system should preserve the exact simulation inputs/results that led to the registration so the internal reviewer sees the customer’s original context.

Prudential’s current product lead form similarly captures name, email, telephone, domicile, protection need and preferred contact window, illustrating that a lightweight contact/application capture step is consistent with established insurance-site practice. [4]

## 4. Proposed mechanics

### 4.1 Public journey

#### Step 1 — Discover

Customer lands on the homepage and sees featured products plus a primary CTA such as **Explore Products** or **Calculate Premium**.

#### Step 2 — Browse

Customer filters or scans products by category / need.

Example categories:

- Life protection
- Family protection
- Savings / endowment
- Education
- Critical illness
- Investment-linked

These categories are implementation examples, not a required final product taxonomy.

#### Step 3 — Evaluate product

Product detail page explains:

- value proposition;
- key benefits;
- eligibility;
- coverage/payment terms;
- premium-related assumptions;
- documents;
- CTA.

#### Step 4 — Simulate

Customer enters age, sum assured, and payment term.

System validates the combination against the product’s configured rule set.

If valid, the system calculates an illustrative premium.

If invalid, the UI explains why and offers a correction.

#### Step 5 — Convert

Customer selects **Apply Now / Request Consultation / Continue Application**.

The application form is pre-filled with simulation information.

#### Step 6 — Submit

System creates the application in `SUBMITTED` status and displays a reference number.

No policy is issued by this MVP.

---

### 4.2 Admin journey

#### Dashboard

Provide summary counts:

- New / Submitted
- Under Review
- Approved
- Rejected
- Assigned to me
- Unassigned
- Overdue / SLA-at-risk (future-ready; optional MVP)

#### Application inbox

Table columns:

- Application ID
- Submitted date
- Applicant name
- Product
- Estimated premium
- Sum assured
- Owner
- Status
- Last updated

Filters:

- Status
- Product
- Assigned user
- Date range
- Search applicant/application ID

#### Application detail

Show:

- Applicant information
- Simulation inputs
- Simulation result
- Product snapshot at submission
- Consent record
- Current status
- Assigned reviewer
- Timeline/audit history
- Reviewer notes
- Available actions

#### Status actions

`DRAFT → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`

or

`DRAFT → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`

Optional future state:

`UNDER_REVIEW → NEEDS_INFORMATION → UNDER_REVIEW`

For the strict MVP, keep the required state machine small and deterministic.

---

### 4.3 Disposition / approval line

The system supports a manager hierarchy:

`Staff A → Manager B → Manager C → ...`

A case can be assigned to a reviewer, then escalated/approved along the configured reporting line.

The organization tree must not allow cycles such as:

`A → B → A`

The system should validate the manager relationship before any hierarchy change is committed.

The MVP should support configurable approval depth. A simple initial rule is:

- First reviewer evaluates the application.
- If the reviewer has approval authority, they may approve/reject.
- If policy/risk rules require a higher authority, the case is routed to the direct manager.
- Each disposition is recorded in the audit trail.

This keeps the MVP compatible with a future multi-level approval process without hard-coding a fixed number of levels.

## 5. Business rules

### Product rules

- Only `PUBLISHED` products appear publicly.
- Draft products are visible only to authorized admin users.
- Archived products are not available for new applications.
- Product content changes should create an audit trail.
- A product may have one or more simulation rule versions.
- A submitted application stores a snapshot of the selected product and simulation result so later product edits do not rewrite history.

### Simulator rules

- Age must be within configured bounds.
- Sum assured must be within configured bounds.
- Payment term must be one of the product’s allowed terms.
- Premium calculation must use the currently active rate version.
- Simulator output must carry an “illustration / not final quote” disclaimer.
- The API is the source of truth for calculation; the frontend must not calculate the final number independently.

### Application rules

- Applicant cannot submit without mandatory fields and consent.
- Every submission receives a unique application reference.
- A submitted application cannot be silently deleted.
- Status changes require an authorized role.
- Approval/rejection requires a reviewer identity and timestamp.
- Rejection should capture a reason code or note.

### RBAC rules

Suggested roles:

| Role | Main permissions |
|---|---|
| Super Admin | Everything; users, roles, hierarchy, products, simulation rules, applications |
| Product Admin | Product content and simulation configuration |
| Reviewer | View/handle assigned applications; update allowed workflow states |
| Manager | Review direct/indirect team applications; approve where authorized |
| Read-only / Auditor | View applications, products and audit history; no mutation |

Authorization must be checked server-side. UI hiding alone is not security.

## 7. Success metrics

The MVP should validate business conversion rather than operational scale.

Primary metrics:

- Product detail → simulator conversion rate
- Simulator completion rate
- Simulator → application conversion rate
- Application submission completion rate
- Percentage of submitted applications assigned to an owner
- Median time from submission to first review
- Approval / rejection ratio
- Product pages generating the most qualified applications

Secondary metrics:

- Most-used sum assured bands
- Most-selected payment terms
- Abandonment by funnel step
- Invalid simulator combinations

## 8. Benchmark-derived product-page checklist

Based on the reviewed Allianz, AXA and Prudential pages, the MVP should make these easy to find on each product:

- Product name and positioning
- Product type / purpose
- Benefit highlights
- Coverage / sum assured
- Entry-age / eligibility rules
- Coverage duration
- Payment term
- Payment frequency
- Minimum/maximum premium where applicable
- Underwriting note where applicable
- Product documents such as brochure / RIPLAY
- Contact/application CTA
- Terms / disclaimer
- Insurer identity and regulatory disclosure

This is a benchmark-informed content model, not a statement that every insurer must expose every field in the same visual format.

## 9. Compliance and trust considerations for MVP

OJK Regulation No. 8/2024 addresses insurance products and insurance marketing channels, including product information, premium/contribution calculation, digital insurance operations, product approval/reporting, and related governance. Historical OJK marketing guidance also explicitly references application procedures, claims information, simulation/illustration, complaint procedures, company contact information, and applicable terms/conditions. [5][6]

Accordingly, this MVP should:

- distinguish illustration from final premium/underwriting;
- show applicable terms and conditions;
- expose insurer/contact identity;
- record customer consent;
- keep an audit trail for material admin actions;
- avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive information at the public discovery stage;
- keep production regulatory, privacy, security and underwriting reviews as release gates.

The MVP is therefore a **business-process prototype**, not a declaration that the implementation is production-compliant.
